# Worked example: "Taiwan's Best Job" (2012) — a real domino chain, read backwards

> **Why this one.** This is the campaign the Butterfly Effect Designer is distilled from. A travel-booking startup with NT$1.5M (≈ US$50k) of runway left turned that money into a job contest instead of ads, and let the applicants, the B&Bs, a job bank and the press do the marketing. Below is the chain written the way the agent writes every chain: one card to push, the two-column board, who pays, what falls next — with every number graded.
>
> **Evidence key**: **A** official statistics / papers · **B** mainstream press · **C** organizer-reported (the strategist's own course slides) or estimate.

---

## The one card that was pushed

A job ad on a job bank:

> **"Taiwan's Best Job" — 1 person, 6 months, 100 B&Bs around the island, NT$1.5M salary. Apply with your CV and a 60-second video.**

The client (the booking site) pushed this one card on **22 Feb 2012** (C). Everything below fell on its own.

## 0. Two-column board (as it actually ran)

**Execution side** — the organizer could run these without anyone else saying yes:

| # | Card | when | judge_number (actual) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Job listing live on the job bank; CV + detailed profile = stage-1 filter | 22 Feb 2012 | ≈ 9,328 applications in about a month (B); ~0.01 % final hit rate (B) |
| E2 | Stage 2: 60-second self-intro video | Mar 2012 | 506 videos submitted (C) — each one a free ad for the campaign |
| E3 | Jury picks 50; public vote opens on the 50 | Apr 2012 | vote pulls the applicants' friends and family in (C) |
| E4 | Top 50 do an overnight "writer's stay" at a B&B and publish | Apr–May 2012 | 12 finalists chosen on vote + review + host satisfaction (C) |
| E5 | 12 finalists tour the island; daily posts | May–Jun 2012 | continuous coverage window (C) |
| E6 | Winner announced, book of the stays published | 4 Jul 2012 (B) | 10,000 new site members during the campaign; **60,000+ fans by the end of the run**, with the organizer reporting over a million daily visits at peak (C) |

**Planning side** — cards that needed someone else to say yes (accelerators, not prerequisites):

| # | Partner | What they gave | What they got | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 1111 Job Bank (the #2 job bank — the #1 had the least to gain) | the listing slot, its traffic, and its *trust* (a job ad is believed where a contest is not) | a story, and the most-watched listing of the year | yes |
| P2 | ~50 B&Bs | NT$30,000 each as a "sponsor host" | first dibs on being written about; fans arriving before the winner did | yes — 50 × 30k = NT$1.5M, the prize fully recouped (C) |

The execution column ran even if P2–P4 had said no; they only made it cheaper and louder.

## 1. Precise audience, and the cheaper source of the same people

- **Who the site needed**: people who travel domestically and book B&Bs online.
- **Who has the most *time* to travel**: people between jobs. So the cheapest, biggest pool of "people with time" is not a travel site's audience — it is a **job bank's** audience. That is why the first card was a job listing, not a travel promotion.

## 2. The equals chain (the lateral jumps that made it work)

`travel = free time = between jobs = job-seeker traffic = a job bank's front page = applicants with cameras = 506 free videos = a public vote = every applicant's friends = B&B hosts who want writers = 50 sponsors paying NT$30k = the prize money = press that writes "9,000 applied" = gear brands wanting the finalists = a book = 60,000 fans = an exit`

Three jumps worth naming:
1. **Prize money = ad budget.** NT$1.5M as a *salary* bought more attention than NT$1.5M as ads would have — and it was recouped by the hosts.
2. **The vote is the distribution.** Voting on 50 people pulls each one's circle in; "travel is rarely done alone, so open a vote and the people around them show up" (C, organizer's own rule).
3. **The finalists are the content team.** Fifty writers doing overnight stays produced the site's first real inventory of reviews, for free.

## 3. Role conversion — the same person monetized five times

applicant (free traffic) → video maker (free ad) → voter magnet (brings friends) → B&B reviewer (content) → finalist on tour (daily coverage) → winner on a 6-month contract (NT$250k/month, C) → book author.

Someone different pays at each step: the job bank (traffic), the hosts (prize), the brands (gear), the publisher (book).

## 4. The first event's three scores (heaven-time / geography / people)

| | Reason | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **Heaven-time** (trend) | Rode the afterglow of Australia's 2009 "Best Job in the World" (≈ 34–35k applicants from ~200 countries; budget ≈ A$1.2–1.7M; earned media valued at A$200M+ by Tourism Queensland, later A$430M; three Cannes Grand Prix — A/B) | 4 |
| **Geography** (gap) | Nobody in Taiwan had run it; the format was proven abroad and empty at home | 5 |
| **People** (willingness) | Low barrier (CV + 60-second phone video), huge upside, and *the job itself was the fantasy* | 5 |

## 5. Desire push table (why each card tipped)

| Card | Main desire (secondary) | Push line | Contact area (sides that win) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 job ad | being seen (bargain) | "Get paid to travel for six months." | 3 — applicant / job bank / site |
| E2 video | being seen (winning) | "Sixty seconds. Show us why it's you." | 2 — applicant / site |
| E3 vote | winning (herd, face) | "You're in the top 50. Your friends decide." | 4 — applicant / friends / hosts / site |
| E4 stays | face (reciprocity) | "Sleep there, write it, the host is watching." | 3 — writer / host / site |
| E5 tour | authority (identity) | "Twelve of you, the whole island." | 4 — finalists / hosts / brands / press |
| E6 book + winner | identity | "Your name on the cover." | 3 — winner / publisher / site |

No desire runs three cards in a row; rational (ranking, top-50 cut) alternates with emotional (friends voting, your name on a cover).

## 6. The seven system-effect questions, answered on the real run

1. **Butterfly — smallest trigger**: one job listing. Cost: the listing slot the partner gave away.
2. **Tipping 25 %**: the first closed group to flip was the top 50 — once 50 people were campaigning for votes, the public followed. (Centola 2018, A: a committed 25 % flips a norm; 21 % does not.)
3. **Matthew / reflexivity**: the published number was *applications*, and "9,000 people applied for one job" became the press hook that drove more applications.
4. **Lollapalooza**: the first card stacked five desires — being seen, winning, face, bargain, herd. More than three, so no ad budget was needed.
5. **Backfire + second-order anger**: hosts overwhelmed by visiting fans; 38 of the top 50 not making the final 12; voters feeling gamed. The organizer's answer was to keep the jury in the loop (vote + review + host satisfaction), not vote alone.
6. **Heat cycle**: ~4.5 months from listing to winner (22 Feb → 4 Jul 2012), with content refreshed by each stage. The long-tail cards: the book, the reviews, the fan base that kept growing after the winner was named — 10,000 members during the run, 60,000+ fans by the end.
7. **Evidence grades**: applications (B), 50 → 12 → 1 (B), 506 videos (C), 50 × NT$30k (C), 10,000 members during the run and 60,000+ fans at the end (C), "over NT$10M" placement (C), later acquisition by a Japanese travel group for ≈ NT$100M (C, organizer-reported — not independently verified).

---

## What the agent would have flagged if it had run before launch

- **One evidence grade too low**: the "506 videos = NT$15M of production saved" line is the organizer's valuation (C). The agent would keep the count and drop the valuation.
- **A prize that is never the client's money**: 50 hosts × NT$30k. That card is the whole model, and it is the one most people copying the Australian format forget.
- **Pick the #2 partner, not the #1**: the second-ranked job bank had the most to gain from an exclusive story. The agent's rule: *the partner with the biggest gap says yes fastest.*
